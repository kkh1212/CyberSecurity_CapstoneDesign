Scope
The standard covers encryption of data at rest and in transit, full-disk encryption on endpoints, TLS for services, and the generation, storage, rotation, and retirement of keys. It applies to all systems handling Confidential or Restricted data and to the teams that operate cryptographic infrastructure.

Roles and Responsibilities
Cryptography Services operates the HSM, manages the key lifecycle, and enforces dual control, while designated key custodians participate in sensitive operations under that control. The Information Security team approves key export or recovery and reviews cryptographic standards. System and application owners apply the required encryption settings and request certificates only from the approved internal authority. All teams handling Confidential data are responsible for ensuring it is encrypted in line with this standard.

Standard Procedure
Confidential data is encrypted at rest with AES-256 and in transit with TLS 1.2 or higher, and laptops and mobile devices use managed full-disk encryption. Keys are generated, stored, rotated, and retired through a hardware security module (HSM), with data-encryption keys rotated annually and key-encryption keys every two years or after a suspected compromise. Key custodians operate under dual control.

Required Information
A request to export or recover key material must identify the key, the system it protects, the business reason, and the requesting custodians. Because no single person may hold complete key material alone, requests must name the second custodian who will participate under dual control.

Approval or Review Process
The security team reviews and approves any key export or recovery request, and dual control is enforced for the operation itself. TLS certificates are issued only from the approved internal certificate authority, and self-signed certificates are prohibited in production.

Exceptions and Escalation
Where a legacy system cannot meet the encryption standard, a time-bound exception with compensating controls and a remediation plan may be approved. Suspected key compromise triggers immediate rotation, revocation of affected certificates, and escalation to the security team.

Records and Retention
Key operations, rotations, custody changes, and approvals are logged and retained according to operating standards to support audit and incident investigation. Certificate issuance and expiry are monitored, and records are kept in line with compliance obligations.

Compliance, Training and Review
Engineers handling cryptographic systems receive role-specific training, and the standard is reviewed at least annually and after relevant incidents. Key operations, rotations, custody changes, and certificate issuance are logged and audited to confirm dual control and timely rotation. Certificate expiry is monitored to prevent outages. Suspected key compromise triggers immediate rotation and revocation, and audit findings drive remediation of any legacy systems operating under time-bound exceptions until they meet the standard.

Contact
Cryptography Services owns this standard with the Information Security team. Requests are submitted through the internal system, and the standard, certificate request process, and key custodian roster are documented on the company intranet policy page.
