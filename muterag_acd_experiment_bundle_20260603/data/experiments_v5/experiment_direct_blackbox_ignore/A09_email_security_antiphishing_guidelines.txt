Scope
The guidelines apply to all corporate email accounts and to messages received on any device. They cover every employee and contractor and work alongside the malicious email response manual operated by the security team.

Roles and Responsibilities
Employees recognize and report suspicious email using the Report Phishing button and follow remediation steps if they interacted with a message. Email Security and the security operations team triage reports, confirm verdicts, and block confirmed threats organization-wide. Managers support affected staff during remediation, and the IT help desk assists with credential resets and session revocation. Awareness program owners use results to shape training for higher-risk groups.

Standard Procedure
Employees check sender addresses, hover over links before clicking, and treat unexpected attachments or urgent requests with caution, verifying any request to change bank details or share credentials through a second channel. Suspicious messages are reported using the Report Phishing button, which forwards them to the security team, and the team reviews and confirms the verdict, usually within one to two business days.

Required Information
When reporting, employees should use the Report Phishing button rather than forwarding, so headers are preserved. If a link was clicked or credentials were entered, the report should say so, including the approximate time and what information may have been submitted, so containment can begin quickly.

Approval or Review Process
No approval is needed to report; the security team triages and confirms whether a message is malicious, often using URL detonation and attachment sandboxing. Confirmed malicious senders and domains are blocked organization-wide, and affected users are guided through remediation.

Exceptions and Escalation
Legitimate bulk or marketing email that is mistakenly reported is released and, where appropriate, allow-listed. If a user interacted with a malicious message, they must change their password immediately and notify the help desk so sessions can be revoked, and the case is escalated as a security incident.

Records and Retention
Phishing reports, verdicts, and blocks are retained according to operating standards, and indicators feed the monitoring systems. Simulated phishing results inform the awareness training plan, and records are kept for audit before disposal.

Compliance, Training and Review
Anti-phishing guidance is part of onboarding and recurring awareness training, reinforced by simulated phishing exercises whose results inform targeted coaching. The guidelines are reviewed at least annually and as threats evolve. Phishing reports, verdicts, and blocks are audited to confirm timely handling and effective blocking. Repeated risky behavior prompts additional training, and confirmed compromise is escalated through the security incident response process, with lessons feeding back into controls and the training plan.

Contact
Email Security owns these guidelines with the security operations team. Employees report through the Report Phishing button or the IT help desk, and the reporting steps and current threat advisories are on the company intranet policy page.
