Scope
The policy applies to all documents and structured data created or held by the company in any format, electronic or physical. It covers every employee and contractor who authors, edits, or handles company information, and it aligns with the data export, encryption, and records retention standards.

Roles and Responsibilities
Authors classify and label the documents they create, and data owners decide the correct level and approve changes for their information. The responsible department reviews and approves classification changes, and the Information Security team approves downgrades from Confidential or higher. Records Management maintains the policy, the labeling guide, and the quarterly review process, while all employees are responsible for honoring handling rules appropriate to each level they encounter.

Standard Procedure
The company uses four levels, Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted, each defining who may access the document and how it must be stored, shared, and disposed of. Authors apply a visible classification label in the header or footer and in file metadata; unlabeled documents are treated as Internal by default. To assign or change a level, the document owner submits a review request through the internal system.

Required Information
A classification or reclassification request should identify the document, the proposed level, the rationale, and the audience that needs access. For downgrades, the owner must explain why the sensitivity has decreased and confirm that no regulated or contractual data remains that would require a higher level.

Approval or Review Process
The responsible department reviews and approves classification changes, and downgrades from Confidential or higher require additional security approval to prevent inappropriate exposure. Quarterly reviews confirm that labels still match content sensitivity, and mismatches are corrected through the same request process.

Exceptions and Escalation
Mixed-sensitivity documents are classified at the highest level present unless sensitive portions are separated. Disagreements about a level are escalated to the data owner and, if needed, the Information Security manager. Suspected mislabeling that has caused exposure is handled as a security incident.

Records and Retention
Classification decisions, review requests, and approvals are retained according to operating standards to support audits. Restricted documents are access-logged, and disposal of classified material, by shredding or cryptographic erasure, is recorded. Retention follows legal and operational requirements.

Compliance, Training and Review
Classification expectations are covered in security awareness training, and the policy is reviewed at least annually. Quarterly reviews confirm that labels still match content sensitivity, and audits sample documents to verify correct labeling, storage, and disposal. Mislabeling that causes exposure is treated as a security incident, and recurring errors prompt targeted training. Disposal of classified material is recorded, and review findings feed updates to the level definitions and handling rules so that protection stays aligned with business needs.

Contact
Records Management and the Information Security team jointly own this policy. Employees submit classification requests through the internal system, and the labeling guide and level definitions are published on the company intranet policy page.
